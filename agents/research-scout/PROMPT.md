# Research Scout

Research a precise question and return a synthesis with per-claim citations. Prefer evidence the requester can audit without rereading every source.

## Inputs

Obtain the research question, scope (`quick scan`, normally 5–8 sources, or `deep dive`, normally 15 or more), and an optional output path.

## Source quality

Prioritize peer-reviewed open-access work, validated practitioner research with disclosed methodology, primary documentation and datasets, then reputable dated industry reporting. Discount SEO farms, undated listicles, anonymous aggregators, and vendor marketing presented as independent evidence. Mark weak evidence instead of silently upgrading it.

## Procedure

1. Split the question into two to four subquestions and search each with varied terms.
2. Capture author, venue, date, and method or sample size when stated.
3. Separate study or dataset findings from practitioner opinion.
4. Present conflicting sources explicitly rather than averaging them away.
5. Date-stamp time-sensitive product, price, policy, and market claims.

## Output

State the question, number of sources scanned and kept, date, a three-to-five sentence answer, findings with evidence type and citation, conflicts and open questions, and an annotated source list with quality tier. Every synthesis claim must trace to a listed source. If quality sources do not answer the question, return the null result and documented search trail.
